## Usage

```tsx
import { AsciiCanvas } from "@/components/asciicn/ascii-canvas";

export function Playground() {
  return <AsciiCanvas brushSize={2} decay={2.5} className="h-96 w-full" />;
}
```

The pad keeps a grid of ink, one value per sample. Every 60th of a second the ink fades, spreads to its neighbours and drifts on a slow, curling flow. The tones go through the charset like any other field, so the brightest ink draws the densest glyph and a fading tail thins out to dots.

Dragging lays ink along the path. A mouse also paints faintly when it just moves over the pad, unless motion is off. A click or tap drops a ripple: a ring that spreads across a water surface over the ink, bends the trails as it passes and glints on its crest.

Each stroke takes the next colour from `inks`. Where strokes overlap, their colours mix.

When nobody has touched the pad for four seconds, a slow ghost brush draws on its own, and drops a ripple every few seconds. Turn it off with `idle={false}`. The pad always opens with a short stroke already on it, so it is never blank.

## Notes

- The pad takes focus with Tab. The arrow keys paint from the centre, Shift paints in longer steps, and Space or Enter sends a ripple. The clear button is a real button.
- Touch paints too. The pad sets `touch-action: none`, so dragging on it doesn't scroll the page; scroll from outside it.
- With reduced motion, or `animate={false}`, painting still works, but ink stays where you put it: no fading, spreading, ripples or ghost brush. Clear empties it at once.
- The loop runs at up to 60 fps and pauses offscreen and in hidden tabs. The idle brush only draws while the pad is on screen.
- The simulation runs on the CPU over the sample grid. A 700 × 400 pad is about 3,000 samples in the classic charset and 25,000 in Braille, well within a frame.
- Resizing the pad, or switching between charsets with different sample grids, starts a fresh pad.
