asciicn draws pictures with characters. It is a WebGL2 and Canvas2D engine that turns a tone into a glyph, about 80 shadcn components and 12 animated scenes built on it, an ASCII Studio for tuning a look, and an agent skill that teaches your coding agent to build new effects on the same engine.

Every component draws real glyphs: letters, block elements, Braille dots, CP437 and PETSCII characters, katakana. Cells snap to whole device pixels, so the text stays sharp at any zoom and on any screen.

## What you get

- **The engine.** `ascii-core` installs into `lib/ascii` as plain source: charsets, palettes, cell metrics, the `AsciiGrid` canvas and `mapTones`, which turns a grid of tones into glyphs. `ascii-shader` adds `AsciiShader`, a WebGL2 renderer that draws a GLSL field as glyphs from a glyph atlas, with colour modes, pointer interaction, audio input and CRT effects. `ascii-export` saves any frame as `.txt`, `.ans`, `.svg` or `.png`, and records `.webm`.
- **The components.** Backgrounds, charts, interface pieces, text effects, and image and video converters, each installable with `npx shadcn add`. Browse them in the [catalog](/docs/examples).
- **The scenes.** Twelve full animated pieces, from a black hole to digital rain in a cathedral. See them in the [scenes gallery](/scenes).
- **The Studio.** [/studio](/studio) lets you pick a scene or component, change its charset, colours and CRT effects, and export the frame or the JSX.
- **The agent skill.** A `SKILL.md` and references that teach Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and other agents the engine: which charset suits which picture, how to write a field, where to place it on a page, how to colour it from your theme, and how to check it in a browser. [Installation](/docs/installation) adds it to your project.

## How ASCII art works here

A picture becomes a grid of cells, each one glyph tall and about half a glyph wide. The engine measures a tone for every cell, from 0 (empty) to 1 (full), and picks the glyph whose ink coverage matches: a space, then `.` and `:`, up to `#` and `@`. That list of glyphs is a charset.

Some charsets carry more detail than one tone per cell. Braille glyphs hold a 2x4 grid of dots, so a `braille` cell samples eight points and lights each dot on its own. Quadrant blocks sample 2x2. The `edge` charset reads the direction of the edge under the cell and draws `-`, `/`, `|` or `\` along it.

Colour is separate from the glyph. In `mono` every glyph uses `--ascii-ink`. `truecolor` gives each glyph the colour of its source, `ansi16` snaps it to a terminal's 16 colours, `duotone` maps tone onto a two-or-more stop ramp, and `palette` uses your theme colours.

## Charsets

| Id          | Glyphs                              | Good for                             |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| `standard`  | ` .:-=+*#%@`                        | Photos, classic ASCII art            |
| `dense`     | 70 glyphs from `.` to `$`           | Smooth gradients and faces           |
| `blocks`    | ` ░▒▓█`                             | Solid fills, bars, heavy shapes      |
| `quadrants` | `▘▝▀▖▌▞▛█` and the rest of the 2x2  | Twice the resolution in solid blocks |
| `braille`   | `⠁⠃⠇⡇⣇⣧⣷⣿`, 2x4 dots per cell       | Fine lines, charts, wireframes       |
| `cp437`     | `·•○◘♠♣♥♦▲▼☺☻░▒▓█` from the IBM PC  | BBS and DOS screens                  |
| `petscii`   | `╱╲─│╭╮●◆▄▌█` from the Commodore 64 | Home computer art                    |
| `katakana`  | `ｱｳｴｵｶｷｼﾂｿﾝ` and digits             | Digital rain                         |
| `dots`      | ` ·•●`                              | Halftone print                       |
| `edge`      | `-` `/` `\|` `\` on edges           | Outlines and line drawings           |

Pass `chars="…"` to use your own ramp, from empty to dense. [Charsets](/docs/engine/charsets) shows each one.

## Ask your agent for any ASCII effect

Once the skill is installed, describe what you want and where it goes. Name the page, the mood, the charset if you have one in mind, and anything that must stay readable:

- "Behind the hero on the home page, add slow Braille contour lines in our accent colour. Keep the headline readable."
- "On the status page, draw the last 90 days of uptime as a row of ANSI blocks, green for up and red for incidents."
- "Make the 404 page a green-phosphor CRT terminal that types out the missing path."
- "Turn the team photos on the about page into ASCII portraits that sharpen into the photo on hover."
- "Add a katakana rain background to the pricing section, slower and dimmer than the Matrix."

You don't need to know which component or API fits. The skill sends the agent through four steps:

1. **Set up the engine.** It checks for `lib/ascii` and installs `ascii-core` and `ascii-shader` if they're missing.
2. **Pick the technique.** A GLSL field on `AsciiShader` for anything that moves or varies per cell, an `AsciiGrid` for grids it computes on the CPU (3D, simulations, terminals, charts), and the image sampler for photos and video. It reads the closest example for technique and installs one only when it is exactly what you asked for.
3. **Build the component.** It writes a typed component with props you can tweak, chooses a charset and colour mode that suit the picture, places it behind or inside your content, and colours it from your theme.
4. **Check it in a browser.** It looks at the result in light and dark mode, at desktop and phone widths and with reduced motion, and fixes what doesn't read.

## Principles

- **Real glyphs, crisp.** Every effect draws characters, never pixel dots, and every cell lands on whole device pixels.
- **Your theme, not ours.** Glyphs read your `--primary` and `--background` tokens through the [`--ascii-*` variables](/docs/theming), so they change when your theme changes.
- **Quiet by default.** Motion is slow. Every animated effect shows one finished frame when the visitor prefers reduced motion, and pauses offscreen and in hidden tabs.
- **Real text stays text.** ASCII headings and labels are still HTML, so they can be selected, read aloud and indexed. Decorative canvases are hidden from screen readers.
- **Source you own.** Everything installs as files in your project. You and your agent can read and change all of it.
