## Usage

```tsx
import { HongKong } from "@/components/asciicn/hong-kong";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <HongKong intensity={0.6} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The view looks across Victoria Harbour from Kowloon. At the back, the ridge of Victoria Peak rises with the Peak Tower near the top and lamps scattered up the slope. Slim residential towers climb the hill in two rows. In front of them stand Central Plaza with its glass pyramid, the Bank of China Tower with its white X braces and sloping roof, and Two IFC with its crown of fins. A dense row of waterfront towers runs along the shore.

Windows switch on and off slowly. Some rooftops run bands of colour and some towers have LED strips running up one edge. Now and then a searchlight or a laser switches on and sweeps the sky or the water. Below the shoreline the harbour mirrors all of it, broken into ripples, while a junk under three red sails and a green and white Star Ferry cross in opposite directions. With `interactive` on, the pointer shifts the layers by different amounts, so the waterfront slides further than the Peak.

It draws in `braille` by default, which keeps the X braces and the window grids fine. `standard` and `dense` give a typed look, and `blocks` gives solid shapes. In `truecolor`, the default, glyphs take the scene's own colours on a deep blue night. In `mono` every glyph takes the theme ink or a `phosphor`, and in `duotone` the tone runs through a `ramp`.

`intensity` scales every glyph's tone and darkens its colour. Lower it to keep a headline on top easy to read. In `braille` and `quadrants` the dot threshold drops with it, so a dimmed harbour keeps its outline. `lights` turns the light show down without dimming the windows.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime` and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a deep blue night gradient.
- The harbour draws the skyline a second time for its reflection, so the cost per sample stays about the same above and below the shoreline. `braille` samples eight times per cell; a larger `cell` makes it cheaper. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
