## Usage

```tsx
import { HongKong } from "@/components/asciicn/hong-kong";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <HongKong intensity={0.6} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The view looks across Victoria Harbour from Kowloon. At the back, the long ridge of Victoria Peak runs across the sky as a single dim line over a dark slope, with a few strings of street lights. Below it a dense wall of towers with lit window grids climbs toward the summit. Three towers stand above the wall: Central Plaza on the left, with lit floor bands, a glowing pyramid top and a mast; the Bank of China Tower in the middle, its X braces drawn as white lines on dark glass, its slanted roofs stepping up to twin masts; and Two IFC, the tallest, striped with vertical light and topped with a crown of fins.

Nothing built moves. Only the lights change: a window switches on or off now and then, the red light on the Bank of China mast pulses about once every two seconds, Central Plaza and a few rooftops drift slowly through colours, the reflections shift by a cell or two every few seconds, and the Star Ferry crosses the harbour once every two and a half minutes. The pointer does nothing.

Every building covers whole sample columns and rows, and windows fill whole glyph cells, so edges stay hard and window grids stay square in any charset. It draws in `quadrants` by default, which gives the sharpest outlines and draws the X braces as clean lines. In `quadrants` and `braille` the facades are solid and unlit windows are gaps; in ramp charsets such as `blocks`, `standard` and `dense` the facades and the Peak are faint glyphs and lit windows the densest ones. In `truecolor`, the default, glyphs take the scene's own colours on a deep blue night. In `mono` every glyph takes the theme ink or a `phosphor`, and in `duotone` the tone runs through a `ramp`.

`intensity` dims the scene. Lower it to keep a headline on top easy to read. In `quadrants` and `braille` it dims colours only, because those charsets draw a dot or nothing and a lower tone would erase whole facades. `lights` fades the colour-changing tops to plain white without touching the windows.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime` and stops. At the default of 2 seconds the mast light is lit.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a deep blue night gradient.
- It has no pointer response. An `interactive` prop passed by a gallery is accepted and ignored.
- The scene is one GPU pass with no loops: the rows of towers are looked up by slot. The harbour draws the skyline a second time for its reflection, so its cost follows the number of samples. `braille` samples eight times per cell and `quadrants` four; a larger `cell` makes it cheaper. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
