## Usage

```tsx
import { Miami } from "@/components/asciicn/miami";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <Miami intensity={0.6} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The view is from the surf, looking west at the shore. A low sun sets behind a row of art deco hotels. Each hotel has rounded corners, a stepped crown, a neon tube along its roofline and a neon fin with a sign that lights one segment at a time. A few signs stutter. Palms sway in front, cars drive past with their headlights on, and waves roll in toward the beach under the sun's glitter.

The hotels keep their size when the box changes shape, so a narrow phone screen shows fewer of them rather than squashed ones. With `interactive` on, the default, the layers slide apart as the pointer moves: the water and palms move most, the sky least.

It draws in `standard` glyphs by default. In `truecolor` mode, also the default, every layer keeps its own colours: pastel facades, pink and teal neon, a violet sky. On a light paper the pastels are deepened so they still read. In `mono` mode every glyph takes the ink or a `phosphor`, and in `duotone` mode the scene runs through a `ramp` such as `outrun` or `vapor`.

`intensity` thins every glyph at once. Lower it to around 0.5 under a headline so the text on top stays easy to read.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime` and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden. Without WebGL it shows a CSS gradient in the scene's colours.
- The whole scene is drawn on the GPU in one pass. The palm crowns cost the most per sample, and `braille` samples eight times per cell. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
