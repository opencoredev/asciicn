## Usage

```tsx
import { Miami } from "@/components/asciicn/miami";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <Miami intensity={0.6} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The view is from the water, looking across the bay at Ocean Drive after sunset. A row of art deco hotels stands along the promenade: pastel facades, stepped parapets, eyebrow ledges over the windows, portholes and blade signs, with neon in magenta, cyan and gold along every roofline. Behind them rise the glass towers of Brickell, a few with lit crowns. Two or three palms stand in front as dark silhouettes, and a crescent moon hangs over the skyline.

Nothing built moves, and the pointer has no effect. What changes is slow: windows switch on and off a few at a time, each fading over a couple of seconds and holding its state for a minute or so. The red beacons on the tallest towers pulse once every 1.8 seconds, the stars brighten and dim, the reflections in the bay break and rejoin, and one small boat crosses the water every two to three minutes.

The hotels keep their size when the box changes shape, so a narrow phone screen shows fewer of them rather than squashed ones. Edges and window grids are snapped to the sample grid, so they come out as clean rows and columns of glyphs.

It draws in `quadrants` by default, at a 9px cell. `braille` gives finer dots, and `standard` and `dense` draw the same scene in letters and symbols. In `truecolor` mode, also the default, every part keeps its own colour; on a light paper the colours are deepened so they still read. In `mono` mode every glyph takes the ink or a `phosphor`, and in `duotone` mode the scene runs through a `ramp` such as `outrun` or `vapor`.

`intensity` thins every glyph at once. Lower it to around 0.5 under a headline so the text on top stays easy to read.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime` and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden. Without WebGL it shows a CSS gradient in the scene's colours.
- The whole scene is drawn on the GPU in one pass. In `braille` and `quadrants` each cell takes the colour of its brightest dot, so a neon line one dot thick keeps its colour. The colour pass reads every dot of the cell to do that, so these two charsets cost more per frame than the others. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
