## Usage

```tsx
import { Dubai } from "@/components/asciicn/dubai";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <Dubai intensity={0.6} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The city is seen from the water at night. The Burj Khalifa stands in the middle, more than twice the height of anything around it. Its three wings step back in turn as it climbs, so the setbacks alternate sides, each with a lit ledge, up to a long spire. The faces carry vertical strips of light. To the left stand the two Emirates Towers with their sloped crowns, to the right the towers of the Marina, and on the shore the sail of the Burj Al Arab on its island. The water below repeats the lights as broken, dimmer copies.

Nothing built moves. Only the lights change: the red beacon on the tip pulses about once every two seconds, a window switches on or off now and then, the stars twinkle faintly, the reflections shift by a cell or two every few seconds, the floodlights on the Burj Al Arab drift slowly through soft colours, and a small boat crosses the water once every two minutes. The pointer does nothing.

Every building covers whole sample columns and rows, and windows fill whole glyph cells, so edges stay hard and window grids stay square in any charset. It draws in `quadrants` by default, which gives the sharpest silhouette. In `quadrants` and `braille` the facades are solid and unlit windows are gaps; in ramp charsets such as `blocks`, `standard` and `dense` the facades are faint glyphs and lit windows the densest ones. In `truecolor`, the default, glyphs take the scene's silver, warm window light and red beacon on a night paper. In `mono` every glyph takes the theme ink or a `phosphor`, and in `duotone` the tone runs through a `ramp`.

`intensity` dims the scene. Lower it to keep a headline on top easy to read. In `quadrants` and `braille` it dims colours only, because those charsets draw a dot or nothing and a lower tone would erase whole facades.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime` and stops. At the default of 2 seconds the beacon is lit.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a night gradient.
- It has no pointer response. An `interactive` prop passed by a gallery is accepted and ignored.
- The scene is one GPU pass with one short loop over the seven Marina towers; the other rows of buildings are looked up by slot. The water draws the land a second time for its reflection, so its cost follows the number of samples. `braille` samples eight times per cell and `quadrants` four; a larger `cell` makes it cheaper. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
