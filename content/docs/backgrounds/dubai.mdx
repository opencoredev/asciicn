## Usage

```tsx
import { Dubai } from "@/components/asciicn/dubai";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <Dubai intensity={0.85} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The whole frame is painted in halftone dots. Each dot takes its own colour, and its size carries the light: the smallest dots in the deep blue at the top of the sky and in the shadows between the dunes, middle dots through the rose and violet, and the largest dots on the lit spire, the afterglow and the sunlit dune crests.

The sun has just set to the left, behind the desert. The sky runs from a hazy gold horizon through rose to deep blue, with thin clouds lit from below and the first stars overhead.

The Burj Khalifa rises far above everything else. Its wings step back in turn as it climbs, so the setbacks alternate sides, and it narrows to a long spire. The lower floors already stand in the blue of dusk with a few lit windows; above them the faces turned to the sunset are still gold and amber, and the far face takes rose from the sky. A red beacon pulses slowly on the tip. A cluster of towers stands round its foot in blue haze, with lit windows and warm edges on the side facing the light, and further towers fade into the haze along the horizon, where the city's glow lies.

In front, three rows of dunes run to the bottom of the frame. The faces turned to the sunset are rose and amber; behind each crest a violet shadow widens down the slip face. The far dunes are hazier and paler, and fine ripples cross the near one.

Motion is slow: the beacon pulses, a window turns on or off now and then, dust drifts over the dunes, thin clouds move and stars twinkle faintly. The pointer does nothing.

The Burj stands right of centre at every aspect, and the towers in the middle of the box stay low, so a centred headline sits over open sky. On a tall phone screen it stands near the right edge, beside the text rather than behind it.

It draws in `dots` by default on square 7px cells, so the dots lie in an even lattice, about 200 across a 1440px screen, with `bayer4` dithering between dot sizes for smooth gradients. `square={false}` returns to the usual text cells, twice as tall as wide; `braille` and `quadrants` use them by default. Other ramp charsets such as `standard` or `dense` read the same tones as typed glyphs. In `truecolor`, the default, the scene paints its own dusk-blue paper in both themes. `mono` with a `phosphor` and `duotone` with a `ramp` read the same scene by tone alone.

`intensity` dims the colours in step and shrinks the dots more gently, so the shapes keep their place behind text.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- It ignores the pointer: no parallax and no hover effect. An `interactive` prop passed by a gallery is accepted and ignored.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime` and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a flat dusk gradient.
- The whole scene is one shader pass on the GPU, with one short loop over the twelve towers round the Burj. `braille` samples eight times per cell and `quadrants` four; a larger `cell` makes it cheaper. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
