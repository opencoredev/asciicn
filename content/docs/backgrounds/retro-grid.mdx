## Usage

```tsx
import { RetroGrid } from "@/components/asciicn/retro-grid";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <RetroGrid className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The floor is a grid seen from a low camera, rolling towards you, with a haze at the horizon and the sun's reflection in the middle. Lines stay about one glyph thick at any depth, and where they crowd together near the horizon they fade out instead of turning into moiré. Above it, the sun sits on the horizon with `slits` cut through its lower half.

The `braille` charset is the default: every grid line is a single trail of dots, so the floor stays sharp all the way to the horizon. With `edge` the floor lines come out as `-` across and `/`, `|` and `\` along, each following the perspective, while the sun keeps a fill of ramp glyphs. `blocks` draws the floor as solid bars.

In `truecolor` mode, the default, the grid takes `ink` and the sun runs from `sunsetColor` at the bottom to `sunColor` at the top.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime` and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a CSS horizon line, floor tint and sun.
- Floor samples read the grid three times to keep lines one glyph wide. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
