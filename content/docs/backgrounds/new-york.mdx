## Usage

```tsx
import { NewYork } from "@/components/asciicn/new-york";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <NewYork intensity={0.7} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The view looks at Manhattan from Brooklyn. One World Trade Center stands at the left, a tapering glass tower with a V of lit glass down its middle, a bright parapet and a spire. Right of centre, the Empire State Building rises in setbacks to its floodlit gold crown, mast and antenna. Beside it, the Chrysler Building's crown steps up in lit arches around triangular windows under its needle. Lower Manhattan and Midtown fill in with towers of window grids, with the low Village between them.

The buildings never move. A few windows switch on or off at a time, each on its own slow clock. The red lights on the two spires pulse about once every two seconds, stars brighten and fade, the river's reflections break and shift a sample at a time, and a ferry crosses in about two minutes. Nothing follows the pointer.

It draws in `quadrants` at a 9px cell by default, so every edge and window lands on a 2x2 sub-cell. `braille` gives finer outlines, `blocks` and `dense` solid shapes, and `standard` a classic ASCII look. In `truecolor`, the default, warm and cool window light sits on a night navy. `mono` with a `phosphor` and `duotone` with a `ramp` read the same scene by tone alone.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- The buildings never move and nothing reacts to the pointer. Only lights, stars, the reflections and the ferry change.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime`, with the ferry on the river, and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a gradient in the same colours.
- The whole city is one shader pass on the GPU. The river reflection reads the skyline a second time, and in `quadrants` and `braille` an empty sample also reads its neighbours so cell colours stay true at edges. `braille` samples eight times per cell, so it costs the most. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
