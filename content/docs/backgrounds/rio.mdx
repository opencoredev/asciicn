## Usage

```tsx
import { Rio } from "@/components/asciicn/rio";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <Rio intensity={0.7} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

On the right, Sugarloaf rises straight out of the bay as a granite dome, lit on the side that faces the moon, with the lower, forested Morro da Urca in front of it. A cable runs from the top of Urca to the top of Sugarloaf, and one cable car crosses it: it waits about 12 seconds at each station, then takes about half a minute to cross. On the left, Corcovado climbs out of the forested range to a sharp granite peak, with Christ the Redeemer on the summit in white, arms straight out. Below them, Copacabana curves away along the shore: a string of lamps on the promenade, a wall of apartment blocks with lit windows, and homes on the lower slopes.

Only a few things move. A handful of windows switch on or off, each fading over a couple of seconds and changing only every minute or more. Stars glint faintly. The lamps, Sugarloaf and the moon lay broken reflections on the water, which shift sideways a little as the bay moves. The mountains, the statue and the buildings stay still, and nothing reacts to the pointer.

It draws in `quadrants` by default, with 9px cells. Each glyph splits into a 2x2 grid of samples, so outlines, the statue and window grids land on whole samples and stay sharp. `braille` gives finer, thinner dots, and `blocks`, `standard` and `dense` also work. In `truecolor`, the default, it paints its own colours on a night blue. In `mono`, `duotone` and `palette`, where colour no longer carries brightness, the hills and Sugarloaf fill with ordered dot patterns of different densities so they stay apart.

## Examples

### Behind a headline

`colorMode="duotone"` with the `sodium` ramp turns the scene gold on its night blue, and `intensity={0.6}` dims the colours and thins the fills. The headline sits low, over the open water, so it reads first.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- It ignores the pointer: no parallax and no hover effect.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime` and stops. The default, 28 seconds, catches the cable car halfway up.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a flat night-blue gradient.
- On wide boxes the whole bay fits. On narrow ones the view closes in on the stretch from Corcovado to the moon and draws it smaller, so both peaks stay in view.
- The whole city is one shader pass on the GPU. `braille` samples eight times per cell and `quadrants` four. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
