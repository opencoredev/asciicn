## Usage

```tsx
import { Rio } from "@/components/asciicn/rio";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <Rio intensity={0.85} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The whole frame is painted in halftone dots. Each dot takes its own colour, and its size carries the light: the biggest dots gather in the gold afterglow on the horizon and its reflection, middle dots run through the rose and violet sky, and the deep sky and the shadowed mountains fall to the smallest dots, close to the paper.

The sun has just set between the mountains. Sugarloaf rises out of the bay on the right as a great dark dome that leans towards the lower, forested Morro da Urca beside it. Its flank towards the afterglow catches a rose light and a lit rim, while the face towards you stays in shadow. A cable runs from the top of Urca to the top of Sugarloaf, a fine dark line against the glow, and one cable car's light crosses it: it waits about 12 seconds at each station, then takes about half a minute to cross. On the left, Corcovado climbs out of the Tijuca range, hazier and paler because it stands farther off, to a granite needle with Christ the Redeemer on the summit, small and lit pale by floodlights. Below it, the lamps of Copacabana curve along the shore in a crescent in front of a low wall of apartment blocks, and homes are lit on the lower slopes.

The bay mirrors all of it, darker and cooler, broken into ripples that widen towards you. Under the afterglow runs a long gold reflection, and the beach lamps draw short warm lines down into the water.

Motion is slow: the cable car crosses, the reflections shimmer as the ripples drift, the lamps along the beach twinkle gently, a few windows turn on or off every couple of minutes, thin clouds drift and stars twinkle faintly. The pointer does nothing.

On wide boxes Corcovado and Sugarloaf frame the middle, so the sky over the afterglow stays calm for a headline. On tall boxes the scene is drawn smaller and lower, so both peaks stay in view beneath the headline.

It draws in `dots` by default on square 7px cells, so the dots lie in an even lattice, about 200 across a 1440px screen. `square={false}` returns to the usual text cells, twice as tall as wide. Other ramp charsets such as `standard` or `dense` read the same tones as typed glyphs. `braille` and `quadrants` light a dot only above half tone, so they keep the lit parts (the afterglow, the lights and the reflection) and leave the dark sky and mountains empty. In `truecolor`, the default, the scene paints its own dusk paper in both themes. `duotone` with a `ramp` and `mono` with a `phosphor` read the same scene by tone alone.

`intensity` dims the colours in step and shrinks the dots more gently, so the shapes keep their place behind text.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- It ignores the pointer: no parallax and no hover effect.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime` and stops. The default, 12 seconds, catches the cable car halfway across.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows the dusk as a flat gradient.
- The whole city is one shader pass on the GPU, one sample per dot. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
