## Usage

```tsx
import { Paris } from "@/components/asciicn/paris";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <Paris intensity={0.7} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The tower stands on the far bank of the Seine, lit gold. For part of every 18 seconds white flashes run over it, and a searchlight turns on its top. In front, a stone bridge crosses the river, a bateau-mouche glides past, and a row of Haussmann buildings with zinc roofs, dormers and chimney pots lines the bottom. Clouds drift through a sky that fades from rose at the horizon to deep blue.

On wide boxes the tower sits right of centre, so a headline fits on the left. On tall boxes it stands in the middle. Moving the pointer slides the near layers further than the far ones.

It draws in `standard` glyphs by default. `braille` gives the finest lattice, `blocks` and `dense` solid shapes, and `edge` an outline drawing. In `truecolor`, the default, it paints its own colours on a deep blue. `mono` with a `phosphor` and `duotone` with a `ramp` read the same scene by tone alone.

## Examples

### Behind a headline

`intensity={0.6}` thins the glyphs so white text on the left reads first, and `clouds={0.7}` fills the sky a little more.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime`, mid-sparkle, and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a gradient in the same colours.
- The whole city is one shader pass on the GPU. `braille` samples eight times per cell, so it costs the most. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
