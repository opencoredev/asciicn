## Usage

```tsx
import { Paris } from "@/components/asciicn/paris";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <Paris intensity={0.6} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The Eiffel Tower is drawn to its real proportions: four splayed legs with the great arch between them, the first floor, the curved taper to the second floor, the long third stage to the top floor, and the antenna. Its iron lattice is a cross-hatch of bright bars, lit warm gold. At the tip the beacon turns slowly and shows as a soft glow that swells about every eleven seconds.

On the far bank, Haussmann blocks line up either side of the open Champ de Mars. Each has floors of tall windows, a stone cornice, a zinc mansard with dormers, and chimney stacks on the party walls. The gilded dome of Les Invalides rises behind them. Below the quay the Seine mirrors the tower in gold streaks, and one bateau-mouche with a lit glass cabin takes about two minutes to cross a wide box.

The architecture never moves, and the pointer does nothing. What changes is slow: a few windows turn on or off every few minutes, fading over three seconds, the stars twinkle faintly, the reflection shifts a column at a time, and the boat glides past.

On wide boxes the tower sits right of centre, so a headline fits on the left. On tall boxes it stands in the middle.

It draws in `quadrants` by default, which keeps the silhouette sharp and solid down to a small catalog tile. `braille` traces the finest lattice, `standard` and `dense` give a typed look, and `blocks` fills shapes in solid shades. Every part is laid out on the engine's sample grid, so edges and window rows land on whole samples in any charset. In `truecolor`, the default, glyphs take the scene's own colours on a deep blue. `mono` with a `phosphor` and `duotone` with a `ramp` read the same scene by tone alone.

`intensity` dims the scene: colours fade in step with it. In ramp charsets the glyphs also thin out, more slowly. In `quadrants` and `braille` every dot stays, so the tower and the roofs keep their shape.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- It ignores the pointer: no parallax and no hover effect.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime` and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a flat blue gradient.
- The whole city is one shader pass on the GPU. Water samples also draw the mirrored tower and boat, so the river costs about twice as much as the sky. `braille` samples eight times per cell and `quadrants` four. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
