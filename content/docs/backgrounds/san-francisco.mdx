## Usage

```tsx
import { SanFrancisco } from "@/components/asciicn/san-francisco";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate flex min-h-[480px] flex-col items-center justify-center text-white">
      <SanFrancisco intensity={0.7} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The view is side on across the water at blue hour, just after sunset. The bridge fills the middle: two Art Deco towers in International Orange, each with two legs, four portal struts above the deck, stepped tops and X bracing below the deck, standing on concrete piers. The main cable hangs from saddle to saddle and dips almost to the road at mid-span, with a suspender every third column, and the side spans run down to the anchorages. The Marin Headlands rise on the left, the low hills of the Presidio sit on the right, and a thin far shore runs along the horizon with the city's lights on it.

The whole frame is painted. The sky runs from deep blue at the top through mauve to a warm afterglow low behind the bridge, with thin streaks of high cloud. A bank of fog lies under the deck and piles up against the headlands, lit warm on top and blue grey underneath. The bay below mirrors all of it, and the afterglow lays a path of glints on the water. Dark areas stay quiet: the deep sky, the fog's shadow and the far water draw the smallest dots in colours a shade above the paper, and the large bright dots are kept for the bridge, the glow and its reflection.

Nothing built moves, and the pointer has no effect. The motion is slow: the fog drifts east under the deck, ripples break the reflections into streaks, the red aircraft lights on the tower tops blink softly out of step with each other, a few stars twinkle, and one boat crosses the bay every few minutes.

The towers move apart as the box gets wider and keep their height. In a portrait box, narrower than about 0.85 wide to 1 tall, the view closes in: the south tower stands right of centre in the lower half, with the main cable sweeping off the left edge, so a headline above it doesn't cover the bridge. Tower legs, struts, the cable and the suspenders are laid out on whole cells, so they come out as clean lines of dots at any size.

It draws in `dots` by default, on square 7px cells so the dots lay an even halftone lattice, with `bayer4` dithering so the dot sizes blend into smooth gradients. In `truecolor` mode, also the default, every dot takes its own colour and the scene paints its own dusk behind them, so it looks the same on light and dark pages. `braille` and `standard` draw the same scene in other glyphs; Braille and quadrants keep their usual tall cells. In `mono` mode every glyph takes the ink or a `phosphor`, and in `duotone` mode the scene runs through a `ramp`, `ember` by default.

`fog` sets how thick the bank is, from none at 0. `traffic` sets how many cars cross the deck as single dots of light, up to six. `boat={false}` takes the boat off the water. `intensity` thins every dot at once. Around 0.7 keeps the bridge clear behind a headline; lower it under body text.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime` and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden. Without WebGL it shows a CSS gradient in the scene's colours.
- The whole scene is drawn on the GPU in one pass, and the water reads the scene above it a second time for the reflection. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
