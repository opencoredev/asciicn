## Usage

```tsx
import { Tokyo } from "@/components/asciicn/tokyo";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <Tokyo intensity={0.6} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The scene is built in layers. A few stars and the snowy cone of Mount Fuji sit at the back, then a low far skyline, Tokyo Tower in red-orange and white with its two observation decks, mid towers with lit window grids, and near blocks hung with vertical neon signs. In front, a train runs along a curving elevated line, and cars pass on the street below.

Windows switch on and off slowly, the signs flicker now and then, and the red lights on the tower and the rooftop antennas blink. With `interactive` on, the pointer shifts the layers by different amounts, so near blocks slide further than the horizon.

It draws in `braille` by default, which keeps the tower's lattice and the window grids fine. In `braille` and `quadrants` it dithers with `bayer4`, so the dim facades and Fuji show as sparse dots instead of vanishing below the dot threshold. `standard` and `dense` give a typed look, and `blocks` gives solid shapes. In `truecolor`, the default, glyphs take the scene's red, white and electric blue on a night paper. In `mono` every glyph takes the theme ink or a `phosphor`, and in `duotone` the tone runs through a `ramp`.

`intensity` scales every glyph's tone. Lower it to keep a headline on top easy to read.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime` and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a dark night gradient.
- The whole scene is one GPU pass with no loops over buildings, so its cost depends on the number of samples. `braille` samples eight times per cell; a larger `cell` makes it cheaper. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
