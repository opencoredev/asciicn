## Usage

```tsx
import { Tokyo } from "@/components/asciicn/tokyo";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <Tokyo intensity={0.6} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The view looks west across Minato on a clear, cool night. Tokyo Tower stands at the right, drawn to the real tower's proportions: legs that flare out over an arch, the Main Deck about 45% of the way up and the smaller Top Deck about 75% up, then the antenna with a red light. It is painted in orange and white bands, both decks are lit, and it is the one warm light in the frame. Mount Fuji stands far off at the left, a moonlit cone in cool grey-blue with a snow cap that is brightest on the side facing the moon. Between them, three layers of towers recede into haze: the nearest are darkest and carry the clearest windows, the farthest sit close to the sky in value, their lights barely there.

The sky runs from a pale cyan-grey glow low over the city through teal and blue-grey to near black overhead, with a small moon above Fuji and a few stars. Most of it is drawn in small, dim dots, so it reads as quiet air. Windows are a soft field of muted white and cyan lights with a few pale warm rooms, lit floor by floor, set in a faint cyan haze of city glow; only about one in six burns bright.

Motion is slow and sparse: a few windows switch on or off over tens of seconds, the tower's glow breathes, haze drifts along the foot of the middle towers, stars twinkle, and a train glides along the elevated line about every 40 seconds. Nothing responds to the pointer.

It draws in `dots` by default on square 7px cells, about 200 columns across a 1440px screen, so the dots lay an even halftone lattice and dot size carries tone: small, dim dots in the night sky, the distance and the shadows, and large bright ones kept for the tower, its glow, the moon and the lit snow on Fuji. Dots, `braille` and `quadrants` use `blue-noise` dithering, which blends neighbouring sizes without stripes. In `truecolor`, the default, every dot takes the scene's own colour on a night paper, so it looks the same on light and dark pages. Fuji and the tower stay at about a fifth and four fifths of the width in any box, so a centred headline covers neither, and a phone crop keeps both.

The central band, the middle half of the width from 30% to 65% of the height, holds only sky and haze. `intensity` dims the colours and shrinks the dots, most of all in that band. Lower it to keep text on top easy to read.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime` and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a night gradient.
- Nothing responds to the pointer, and there is no `interaction` prop.
- The whole scene is one GPU pass, so its cost depends on the number of samples. `braille` samples eight times per cell; a larger `cell` makes it cheaper. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
