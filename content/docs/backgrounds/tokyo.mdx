## Usage

```tsx
import { Tokyo } from "@/components/asciicn/tokyo";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <Tokyo intensity={0.6} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The view looks across Minato at night. Tokyo Tower stands on the right, drawn to the real tower's proportions: legs that flare out like the Eiffel Tower's, an open lattice with X bracing below the Main Deck, the Main Deck about 45% of the way up and the smaller Top Deck about 75% up, then the antenna. It is painted in international orange and white bands, and both decks are lit. Mount Fuji sits far off on the left, a symmetric cone with a snow cap, dimmer than the city. A dense row of high-rises fills the bottom with grids of amber, white-cyan and gold windows, and an elevated railway runs in front of them.

The buildings, the tower and Fuji never move, and the pointer does nothing. Only lights change: a few windows switch on or off at a time, each fading over a few seconds; red beacons on the tower and the tallest roofs pulse about once every 1.8 seconds; stars twinkle faintly; and one train crosses the elevated line slowly as a row of lit windows.

Every building edge and window sits on whole samples, so the window grids stay regular in every charset. It draws in `quadrants` by default at a 9px cell, which keeps the tower bold and its colours strong. In `quadrants` and `braille` it uses `bayer4` dithering, which turns Fuji's slopes and the gaps in the lattice into even grids of dots. `braille` draws the finest lattice in lighter dots, `blocks` gives solid shapes, and `standard` and `dense` a typed look. In `truecolor`, the default, glyphs take the scene's own colours on a night paper. In `mono` every glyph takes the theme ink or a `phosphor`, and in `duotone` the tone runs through a `ramp`.

`intensity` dims the colours and thins the glyphs. Lower it to keep a headline on top easy to read.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime` and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a dark night gradient.
- Nothing responds to the pointer, and there is no `interaction` prop.
- The whole scene is one GPU pass with no loops over buildings, so its cost depends on the number of samples. `braille` samples eight times per cell; a larger `cell` makes it cheaper. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
