## Usage

```tsx
import { London } from "@/components/asciicn/london";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <London intensity={0.7} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The view looks across the Thames from the South Bank. The Elizabeth Tower stands right of centre: a floodlit Gothic shaft, the clock stage with its glowing dial and hands, the gilded belfry and a slate spire with gold edges. The Palace of Westminster runs to its left, a long front of lit windows in bays with a pinnacle over every buttress, the Central Tower's lantern and spire, and the square Victoria Tower at the far end. Further down the river, Tower Bridge shows its two towers, the blue walkways between them and the chains sweeping down to each bank, with the Shard behind it.

The buildings never move. A few windows switch on or off at a time, the clock hands keep real time from ten past ten, car lights cross Tower Bridge slowly, the red light on the Shard pulses about once every two seconds and a river boat passes in about two minutes. The embankment lamps drop amber streaks into the water, and the reflections break and shift a sample at a time. A faint haze lies on the water and the bank. It never moves and never crosses a building. Nothing follows the pointer.

It draws in `quadrants` at a 9px cell by default, so every edge and window lands on a 2x2 sub-cell. `blocks` and `dense` give solid shapes, `standard` a classic ASCII look and `braille` fine outlines. In `truecolor`, the default, floodlit gold stone and blue steel sit on a night blue. `mono` with a `phosphor` and `duotone` with a `ramp` read the same scene by tone alone.

### Sodium duotone

`colorMode="duotone"` with `ramp="sodium"` runs every tone through one amber ramp, from shadow to lamp flame. `braille` glyphs draw fine outlines, `grade={{ gamma: 1.7 }}` lifts the stone out of the dark end of the ramp, and `fog={1}` with no boat keeps it quiet.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- The buildings never move and nothing reacts to the pointer. Only lights, stars, the clock hands, the reflections and the boat change.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime`, with the boat on the river, and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a gradient in the same colours.
- The whole city is one shader pass on the GPU. The river reflection reads the skyline a second time, and in `quadrants` and `braille` an empty sample also reads its neighbours so cell colours stay true at edges. `braille` samples eight times per cell, so it costs the most. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
