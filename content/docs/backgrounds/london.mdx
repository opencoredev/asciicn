## Usage

```tsx
import { London } from "@/components/asciicn/london";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <London intensity={0.85} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The whole frame is painted in halftone dots. Each dot takes its own colour, and its size carries the light: small, dim dots in the slate blue at the top of the sky, and larger ones in the pale gold low along the roofline, where the sun has just gone down behind the palace.

The view looks across the Thames at Westminster. The Elizabeth Tower stands to the right: a floodlit shaft, the clock stage with its glowing dial and two hands, the open belfry lit from inside, and a slate spire banded in gold. The dial is the brightest thing in the picture and warms the air around it. The Palace of Westminster runs left from the tower's foot, a long gold front of buttresses and lit windows under a dark roof with gold cresting and pinnacles, with the Central Tower's lantern and the Victoria Tower further off in the haze. Westminster Bridge runs from the tower's foot towards you on green arches, its lamps glowing on their standards.

The Thames mirrors all of it, darker and cooler, broken into long dashes that widen towards you. A gold streak lies under the tower and a thin one under each bridge lamp.

Motion is slow: the reflections shimmer, mist drifts along the horizon and over the water, the lamps and the dial breathe softly, a few windows turn on or off every few minutes, the clock hands keep slow time and one river cruiser takes about three minutes to cross a wide box. The pointer does nothing.

The tower stands right of the middle in every box, so a centred headline sits over the calm sky and the palace, with the tower beside it. On a phone the palace fills the left and the tower the right.

It draws in `dots` by default on square 7px cells, so the dots lie in an even lattice, about 200 across a 1440px screen. `square={false}` returns to the usual text cells, twice as tall as wide; `braille` and `quadrants` use text cells unless you ask for square ones. Other ramp charsets such as `standard` or `dense` read the same tones as typed glyphs. In `truecolor`, the default, the scene paints its own dusk paper in both themes. `mono` with a `phosphor` and `duotone` with a `ramp` read the same scene by tone alone.

`intensity` dims the colours in step and shrinks the dots more gently, so the shapes keep their place behind text.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- It ignores the pointer: no parallax and no hover effect.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime`, with the boat on the river, and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a flat dusk gradient.
- The whole city is one shader pass on the GPU. Water samples also draw the mirrored scene, so the river costs about twice as much as the sky. `braille` samples eight times per cell and `quadrants` four. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
