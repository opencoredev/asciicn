## Usage

```tsx
import { Sydney } from "@/components/asciicn/sydney";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <Sydney intensity={0.6} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The scene is built in layers. Stars sit at the back, then the city: the towers of the CBD, North Sydney across the water, and Sydney Tower with its gold turret. The Harbour Bridge spans in front of them, its arch drawn as two steel chords with the truss between, granite pylons at each end and lamps along the deck. The Opera House stands nearest, its shells lit white on the podium with amber glass in their mouths.

Fireworks rise from the bridge deck and break over the arch, five shells on their own clocks. Some burst as rings, some as peonies, and gold willows droop and glitter as they fall. Each break briefly tints the steel and the sails. With `interactive` on, a click launches one more shell from the deck below the pointer, and the pointer shifts the layers by different amounts, so the Opera House slides further than the skyline.

Below the waterline the whole picture is drawn again from its mirror image and broken into ripple streaks, so the fireworks land in the harbour too. A green and gold ferry crosses with its cabins lit and a wake behind it.

It draws in `braille` by default, which keeps the spark trails and the truss fine. `standard` and `dense` give a typed look, and `blocks` gives solid shapes. In `truecolor`, the default, glyphs take the scene's own colours on a deep navy paper. In `mono` every glyph takes the theme ink or a `phosphor`, and in `duotone` the tone runs through a `ramp`.

`intensity` dims the scene: colours fade in step with it, and glyphs thin out more slowly so the landmarks stay legible. Lower it, and `fireworks` with it, to keep a headline on top easy to read.

## Examples

### Behind a headline

`intensity={0.6}` and `fireworks={0.6}` keep three shells in the air and push the harbour back, so the headline reads first. Click the sky to launch another.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime`, with shells mid-burst, and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a dark night gradient.
- On narrow boxes the landmarks shrink toward the waterline so the Opera House and the arch both stay in view, while the fireworks keep the full sky.
- The whole scene is one GPU pass. Each sample checks the five shells once, whatever the spark count, so its cost depends on the number of samples. `braille` samples eight times per cell; a larger `cell` makes it cheaper. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
