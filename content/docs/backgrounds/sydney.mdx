## Usage

```tsx
import { Sydney } from "@/components/asciicn/sydney";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate flex min-h-[480px] flex-col items-center justify-center text-white">
      <Sydney intensity={0.75} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The harbour is seen from the east at blue hour, just after sunset. The Opera House stands on the left on its podium: two halls of shells in profile, the tallest at the north end, each a curved sail with a pointed tip. Their faces are lit cream and warm, with a cool shadow along each shell's back edge and a dark seam wherever one shell passes in front of another. Glass walls under the tallest tips glow amber, and the restaurant's two small shells sit at the south end above the stairs.

The Harbour Bridge spans the right of the frame: a granite pylon at each end, lit on the face toward the glow, and the arch between its two chords, with a hazy truss, hangers down to the deck and lamps along the arch and the deck. The city's towers stand behind in cool haze, with a needle tower among them and North Sydney past the bridge.

The whole frame is painted. The sky runs from deep blue at the top through lavender to a peach afterglow low between the house and the bridge. The harbour mirrors the sails, the arch and the glow through slow ripples, and the afterglow lays a path of glints on the water. Far things draw smaller, hazier dots than near ones, and only the lit sails, the glow and its reflection draw the largest.

Nothing built moves, and the pointer has no effect. The motion is slow: ripples break and shift the reflections, a few windows switch on or off, fading over a couple of seconds, red lights on the crown of the arch and the needle tower blink softly, stars twinkle, and one green and cream ferry crosses the harbour every few minutes.

The middle of a wide box stays quiet for a headline: the house sits on the left and the bridge on the right. On a tall box both close in and sit low, the tallest shell just right of centre and the arch rising behind it, so a phone shows them under the copy.

It draws in `dots` by default, on square 7px cells so the dots lay an even halftone lattice, with `bayer4` dithering so the dot sizes blend into smooth gradients. In `truecolor` mode, also the default, every dot takes its own colour and the scene paints its own blue hour behind them, so it looks the same on light and dark pages. `braille` and `standard` draw the same scene in other glyphs; Braille and quadrants keep their usual tall cells. In `mono` mode every glyph takes the ink or a `phosphor`, and in `duotone` mode the scene runs through a `ramp`, `ember` by default.

`ferry={false}` takes the ferry off the water. `intensity` thins every dot at once. Around 0.75 keeps the sails clear behind a headline; lower it under body text.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- It ignores the pointer: no parallax and no hover effect.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime` and stops. The default, 30 seconds, puts the ferry on the water below the glow.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a blue-hour gradient.
- The whole harbour is one shader pass on the GPU. Water samples also draw the mirrored scene, so the harbour costs about twice as much as the sky. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
