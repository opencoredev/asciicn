## Usage

```tsx
import { Sydney } from "@/components/asciicn/sydney";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <Sydney intensity={0.6} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The harbour is seen from the east, at night. The Opera House stands nearest, on the left: a broad podium with a lit edge and a row of foyer lights, and on it the shells in profile, pointed sails of different sizes overlapping in sequence, the tallest at the north end. They are lit cream-white, and a dark seam runs wherever one shell passes in front of another, so each one keeps its own outline. The smaller back hall peeks out behind the front one, and the restaurant's two small shells sit at the south end.

Behind it the Harbour Bridge spans the water to the right. Its arch is a steel truss between two curved chords, with hangers down to the deck and lamps along it. Each end has a pair of granite pylons, one either side of the deck, with one face floodlit and the other in shadow. To the left of the Opera House the towers of Circular Quay rise in a cluster, each with a grid of windows, and North Sydney's lower towers stand past the bridge.

Very little moves. A few windows switch on or off, each fading over a couple of seconds and changing only every minute or more. Stars glint faintly. Red aviation lights pulse slowly on the crown of the arch and on the tallest towers, about once every two seconds. One green and cream ferry crosses the harbour in about two and a half minutes. The water carries broken reflections of the lit shells, lamps, windows and steel, which shift a little from side to side. The buildings and the bridge stay still, and nothing reacts to the pointer.

It draws in `quadrants` by default, with 9px cells. Each glyph splits into a 2x2 grid of samples, so the shells, the truss and the window grids land on whole samples and stay sharp. `braille` gives finer, thinner dots, and `blocks`, `standard` and `dense` also work; with one sample per glyph the bridge keeps its chords and every other post. In `truecolor`, the default, it paints its own colours on a night blue. In `mono`, `duotone` and `palette`, where colour no longer carries brightness, the podium, pylons and tower walls fill with ordered dot patterns so they stay apart from the white shells.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- It ignores the pointer: no parallax and no hover effect.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime` and stops. The default, 40 seconds, puts the ferry in front of the Opera House.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a flat night-blue gradient.
- On wide boxes the towers, the Opera House and the whole bridge fit. On narrow ones the view closes in on the Opera House and the south half of the bridge, and draws them smaller.
- The whole city is one shader pass on the GPU. Water samples also draw the mirrored scene, so the harbour costs about twice as much as the sky. `braille` samples eight times per cell and `quadrants` four. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
