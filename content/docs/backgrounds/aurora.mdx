## Usage

```tsx
import { Aurora } from "@/components/asciicn/aurora";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <Aurora className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

Each curtain has a bright lower edge that folds slowly, with rays that fade as they rise. Further curtains hang higher and dimmer. `height` sets where the lowest edge hangs, so you can keep the area under it clear for text.

It draws in `braille` by default: each glyph holds a 2×4 grid of dots, so the edges stay thin and the rays read as fine needles. Switch `charset` to `standard` or `dense` for a coarser, typed look, or to `blocks` for solid bands.

In `truecolor` mode, the default, colour follows height: `ink` at the bright edge, `tipColor` where the rays fade out. In `mono` mode every glyph takes `ink` (or a `phosphor`), and in `duotone` mode the tone runs through a `ramp`.

## Examples

### Above a headline

Raising `height` lifts the curtains off the lower third, where the headline sits on clean paper. A `tipColor` of `"blue"` cools the tops of the rays, and `mask="edges"` fades the sides.

<ComponentPreview src="examples/aurora-hero.tsx" size="lg" align="stretch">
  <AuroraHero />
</ComponentPreview>

### Northern lights

Your own colours make it a night sky: green edges, violet tips and a deep blue `paper`. Four curtains, longer rays, a slight `tilt` and some `drift` add depth.

<ComponentPreview src="examples/aurora-borealis.tsx" size="lg" align="stretch">
  <AuroraBorealis />
</ComponentPreview>

### On a green phosphor monitor

`colorMode="mono"` with `phosphor="p1"` draws every glyph in one CRT green on near-black glass. The `standard` charset and `fx` scanlines, bloom and vignette finish the look.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime` and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a CSS glow along the edge in the same colours.
- Glyphs are drawn on the GPU in one pass. More `curtains` cost a little more per sample, and `braille` samples eight times per cell. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
