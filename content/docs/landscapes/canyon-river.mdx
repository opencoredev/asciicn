## Usage

```tsx
import { CanyonRiver } from "@/components/asciicn/canyon-river";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <CanyonRiver intensity={0.6} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

You look down a desert canyon from its rim just after sunset. Eroded walls rise on both sides with broken rims, buttresses and gullies. Faces turned to the sun are warm rust and orange; faces turned away are cool and dark, with only their rims catching the sky. Behind them a middle wall and two overlapping rows of buttes step back towards a hazy plateau on the horizon, each farther layer paler and softer. The buttes stand at the sides, so the middle stays open.

The sun sits half set behind the plateau with a soft halo. A river leaves it through a notch in the plateau and winds towards you, showing through the gap in each wall in turn. It mirrors the sky: gold under the sun, violet and indigo where you look straight down into it. The sky runs from gold at the horizon through rose and violet to indigo overhead, with soft bands of cloud lit from below and the first stars higher up.

Only light moves. The last light slides slowly along the far rims, streaks of shimmer cross the water, the clouds drift, the glow breathes and the stars twinkle. The pointer does nothing.

On wide boxes the sun sits well right of centre, so the middle stays calm for a headline. On tall boxes the view narrows and the horizon rises, so on a phone the sunset sits above a centred headline and the river runs down beside it.

It draws in `dots` by default: dot size follows brightness, and in `truecolor` every dot takes its own colour, so shadow and the high sky stay a quiet grain of small, dim dots and the largest dots are kept for the sun, its reflection and the sunlit rims. Cells are square by default, so the dots sit on an even halftone lattice about 200 columns across a 1440px screen. With `square={false}` the cells are tall again and `dots` lights every other column to keep the lattice even. The scene paints its own sky on a deep dusk paper, so it looks the same on light and dark pages. Text charsets such as `standard` and `dense` give a typed look and read best with `square={false}`, and `braille` and `quadrants` light a dot only above half tone. `mono` with a `phosphor` and `duotone` with a `ramp` read the same scene by tone alone.

`intensity` dims the scene: colours fade in step with it and the dots shrink a little. It quiets the middle of the box more than the edges, so a centred headline reads first while the walls and the sky still frame it.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- It ignores the pointer: no parallax and no hover effect.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime` and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a flat dusk gradient.
- The whole canyon is one shader pass on the GPU, one sample per cell in `dots`. `braille` samples eight times per cell and `quadrants` four. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
