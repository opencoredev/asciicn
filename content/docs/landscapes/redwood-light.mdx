## Usage

```tsx
import { RedwoodLight } from "@/components/asciicn/redwood-light";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <RedwoodLight intensity={0.6} className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The view is from the forest floor, looking up. Three giant trunks stand in front, two on the left and one on the right, dark red-brown and furrowed, with a warm rim of light on the side that faces the sun. Behind them two rows of thinner trunks recede into blue-green haze, each row paler and cooler than the one in front. Every trunk leans towards one point high above the middle of the box, so the forest reads as seen from below.

The sun shines through an opening in the canopy, upper right, as a small soft halo. Four soft-edged shafts of golden light fall from it, nearly straight down, and thin out into the haze as they reach the fern floor. Fern clumps get smaller and hazier towards the far edge of the floor, and a row of fronds crosses the bottom of the box.

The motion is slow. Dust and pollen drift through the shafts and catch the light, each mote fading in and out. The shafts brighten and dim a little, and a thin mist drifts between the far trunks. Nothing reacts to the pointer.

The middle of the box is calm, dark green air between the trunks. The light stays upper right and the shafts fade where they near the middle, so a centred headline sits on quiet ground. On tall boxes the giants narrow, the halo shrinks and the shafts dim, so a phone shows the light above the headline and the trunks beside it.

It draws in `dots` by default on square 7px cells, about 200 columns across a 1440px screen, so the dots sit on an even lattice like a halftone screen. `square={false}` goes back to the font's narrower cells. Dot size carries the light and `truecolor` gives every dot its own colour, while the shade falls to the smallest dots, close to the paper, or to none. `braille` and `quadrants` draw the same forest in finer dots. In `truecolor`, `ansi16` and `duotone` it paints on its own dark green shade, so it looks the same on the light and dark site themes. `mono` with a `phosphor` and `duotone` with a `ramp` read the scene by tone alone.

`intensity` dims the colours and shrinks the dots together. Around 0.6 the forest falls back behind a headline.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- It ignores the pointer: no parallax and no hover effect.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame at `stillTime` and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a soft gradient of the scene.
- The whole forest is one shader pass on the GPU, one sample per cell in `dots`. `braille` samples eight times per cell and `quadrants` four. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
