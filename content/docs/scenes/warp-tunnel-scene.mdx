## Usage

```tsx
import { WarpTunnelScene } from "@/components/asciicn/scenes/warp-tunnel-scene";

export function Launch() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[520px]">
      <WarpTunnelScene className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1 className="text-white">Ship faster</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

Stars stream out of the vanishing point as thin radial streaks, longer and brighter the closer they pass. Their colour shifts with position: blue near the centre, where you are flying toward them, white abreast, and red at the edges as they fall behind. The lanes twist into a slow spiral, the tunnel walls slide past as ribs and panels, and every few seconds a ring of light races out of the tunnel.

The vanishing point eases toward the pointer, so you steer the flight. A press fires the drive: for about two seconds the streaks stretch, the colours shift further and a ring leaves the centre.

`velocity` sets the flight speed only, while `speed` scales every motion in the scene, including the twist and the rings.

## Examples

### Edge glyphs

The `edge` charset draws the direction of each streak with `- / | \`, so the tunnel becomes a fan of slashes that turn as they go round. Dim `walls` keep the streaks the only edges, and the blueprint phosphor draws them in one cyan ink.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws the `stillTime` frame and stops. It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen.
- The pointer is read from the window, so it works under overlays and text; the canvas itself never takes pointer events.
- Stars live in a grid laid out in log-polar space around the vanishing point, where flying outward is a constant scroll. Each star keeps its cell for its whole flight, so nothing is stored between frames. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
