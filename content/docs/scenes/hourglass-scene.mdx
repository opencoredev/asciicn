## Usage

```tsx
import { HourglassScene } from "@/components/asciicn/scenes/hourglass-scene";

export function Timer() {
  return (
    <div className="relative h-[520px] overflow-hidden rounded-lg">
      <HourglassScene duration={30} className="absolute inset-0" />
    </div>
  );
}
```

The sand is a falling-sand automaton. Every grain is one cell of a square grid sized to the charset's sample grid, so Braille draws one grain per dot and the dense ramp draws two grains per glyph. Each step a grain falls toward gravity, slides down a diagonal when it lands on another grain, and sometimes slips off an edge. That is enough for the sand to hold a slope, so it leaves a crater in the top bulb and a cone in the bottom one. Grains speed up as they fall, so the stream under the neck thins out.

The grid lives in the glass's own frame. When the top bulb is empty, the glass waits a second and turns over in 2.4 seconds. The sand doesn't get copied or flipped. Gravity just turns inside the glass, so you can watch the grains slide around the bulb as it rotates. The neck lets through a fixed number of grains per step, so one pour takes about `duration` seconds on any grid.

Each grain keeps a colour from its layer, and layers are laid down in the order they pour. The bands bend into a funnel above the neck and stack up in the cone below. Every turn reverses the order.

Move the pointer left or right to tilt the glass up to `tilt` degrees, and the sand slides with it. Press to shake it: the glass rattles and grains jump loose for a moment, which levels the piles.

## Notes

- The canvas is `aria-hidden`; it is decoration.
- The first frame is built mid-pour: about 35% of the sand is already in the bottom cone, and a moment of simulation fills the stream. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` and with `animate={false}` the scene draws that frame and stops.
- The sand steps at a fixed rate tied to the glass's size in cells, up to eight steps a frame, so it falls at the same pace on any grid and frame rate.
- The sand grid is sized from the box and the charset. A resize or a charset change builds a new grid at the same point in the pour, so the glass carries on rather than starting over.
- Upright, the glass fills about 92% of the box's height, or less in a narrow box. While it turns it scales down to fit, so you always see the whole glass.
- The simulation holds at most about 34,000 cells. If the box is very large, grains grow past one dot each instead of the grid growing.
