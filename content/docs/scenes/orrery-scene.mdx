## Usage

```tsx
import { OrreryScene } from "@/components/asciicn/scenes/orrery-scene";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[520px]">
      <OrreryScene className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1 className="text-white">Built to keep time</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

A bronze plate sits under a glowing sun, seen from above at a three-quarter angle. A bronze 12-tooth centre wheel turns on the central column, and four brass gears with 6, 8, 9 and 10 teeth ring it. All five are cut with the same tooth size, so each follower sits one pitch radius from the centre wheel's edge and turns the other way at the ratio of their tooth counts: the 6-tooth pinion turns twice for every turn of the centre wheel. Their teeth stay interleaved where they touch.

Three arms rise from the central column at different heights. The inner one carries a small red planet, the middle one a banded planet with a tilted ring, and the outer one a blue planet with a moon on its own short arm. The further out an arm reaches, the slower it turns.

Gear faces are shaded like spun brass: a bright streak crosses each wheel toward the light and stays put while the teeth run through it. Tooth rims catch the light, tooth gaps and spoke windows fall dark, and the gears cast soft shadows on the plate. Orbit lines are engraved into the plate and a ring of hour ticks into the bezel.

Dragging sideways across the scene winds the clockwork faster. Drag the other way and it runs backwards. Let go and it coasts back to its own pace over a second or two. Hovering tilts and turns the view a little toward the pointer. `tilt` sets the viewing angle and `engraving` how deep the cut lines read.

## Examples

### Edge glyphs

The `edge` charset draws `- / | \` along strong tone edges. Bright brass against the dark plate gives every tooth flank its own stroke, so the gear outlines turn into rings of slashes and bars that rotate with the train.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- The canvas ignores the pointer, so links and buttons on top of it keep working. Drags are read from the window and count only when they start inside the scene.
- With reduced motion turned on it draws one still frame, at `stillTime`, and stops. Dragging does nothing then.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and gives up its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL2 it shows `fallback`, a gradient of the sun over the plate.
- Each sample marches its ray through the instrument at up to 96 steps, then traces a soft shadow and four occlusion taps. Rays that miss the instrument's bounding sphere skip all of it. Braille samples eight times per glyph; a larger `cell` or a single-sample charset cuts the work. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
