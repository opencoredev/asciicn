## Usage

```tsx
import { TorusKnot } from "@/components/asciicn/torus-knot";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative h-[520px]">
      <TorusKnot p={3} q={7} className="absolute inset-0" />
    </section>
  );
}
```

A torus knot is a closed curve drawn on the surface of a ring. It winds `p` times around the ring's axis and passes `q` times through its hole. When `p` and `q` share no common factor you get one knot, such as the trefoil at `p={2} q={3}`. When they do, you get a link of several loops.

The tube is a real 3D mesh. It is swept along the curve, projected in perspective and rasterised into a depth buffer at the charset's own sample grid, so Braille gets 2×4 samples per cell and quadrants 2×2. Each sample is lit from its surface normal: a key light sets the tone, a tight highlight reaches the densest glyphs, and a rim brightens the edges. Parts farther from the camera fade, which puts the near strands in front.

In truecolor the colour comes from `colors`, the site's orange, pink, purple and blue by default. With `hue="film"` it shifts with the surface angle like oil on water, so the colour slides over the tube as it turns. With `hue="tube"` it runs along the knot instead.

Where one strand crosses over another, the strand underneath breaks just short of the one on top, the way knots are drawn by hand. That keeps every crossing readable even in a coarse charset. With ramp charsets, `contours` traces the silhouette with `- / | \` glyphs that follow the outline.

It turns on its own axis and sways a little, so you see the knot near face on with its depth showing. Drag to tilt it. Let go mid-drag and it coasts, then stays where you left it. With keyboard focus, the arrow keys turn it.

## Examples

### Braille, coloured along the tube

`hue="tube"` lays the `colors` stops along the tube and loops back to the first, so you can follow one strand round the whole knot. Braille samples 2×4 dots per cell, which gives a (3, 4) knot a fine outline and an even dot shading.

## Notes

- It is announced as an image whose label names the knot. Pass `aria-label` to describe what it stands for on your page.
- Under reduced motion it draws one still frame. Dragging and the arrow keys still turn it.
- It draws at up to 60 fps on the CPU and pauses offscreen and in hidden tabs. The mesh is built once per shape; each frame only transforms it and fills the samples it covers. Large boxes in Braille cost the most, so raise `cell` for a full-screen hero.
