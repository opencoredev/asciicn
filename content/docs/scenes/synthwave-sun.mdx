## Usage

```tsx
import { SynthwaveSun } from "@/components/asciicn/synthwave-sun";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate h-[560px]">
      <SynthwaveSun className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
    </section>
  );
}
```

The scene runs on the GPU as one field. The floor is a perspective grid: rows race toward you and columns meet at the vanishing point. A line lights exactly the samples it passes through, so in Braille every grid line is one dot wide and in a glyph ramp one glyph wide. Far from the camera the rows crowd closer than one sample, and there they fade into an even haze instead of flickering.

The sun sets into the horizon in amber at the top and magenta below. Slices cut across its lower half in whole sample rows, so each gap is a clean row of empty cells. They widen as they fall and slide slowly down. A one-row line marks the horizon, the glow above it thins as it climbs, and the sun's reflection breaks into alternate rows on the floor.

Move the pointer across it to steer: the vanishing point, the sun and the grid's columns swing with you. Set `interactive={false}` to keep it centred.

## Examples

### Behind a heading

Lower `mountains` and `travel` for a calmer backdrop behind text. The heading is real text on top; the scene sits behind it at `-z-10`.

### Quadrants on an amber phosphor

`phosphor="p3"` turns it mono on amber glass, and `fx` adds bloom and scanlines. Quadrants split each cell 2×2, which keeps the slices sharp at a larger cell.

## Notes

- The canvas is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put your real heading and copy beside or on top of it.
- Under reduced motion it draws one still frame at `stillTime`. It pauses offscreen and in hidden tabs.
- On a light page the colours deepen so amber stays visible on white paper.
- Without WebGL2 it shows `fallback`: by default a gradient with the sun, the sky and the horizon.
