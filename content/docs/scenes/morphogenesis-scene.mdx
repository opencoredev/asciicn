## Usage

```tsx
import { MorphogenesisScene } from "@/components/asciicn/scenes/morphogenesis-scene";

export function Specimen() {
  return (
    <div className="relative h-[520px] overflow-hidden rounded-lg">
      <MorphogenesisScene className="absolute inset-0" />
    </div>
  );
}
```

The scene runs a Gray-Scott reaction-diffusion model on the CPU. Two chemicals share a grid of square cells, up to 40,000 of them, about 2.6 CSS pixels across for Braille and quadrants and 5.7 for the other charsets. Chemical A feeds in everywhere; chemical B consumes it to copy itself and slowly decays. The feed and kill rates decide what B grows into: branching coral, fingerprint whorls, a labyrinth, or spots that split in two.

With `pattern="drift"`, two slow waves move across the box and set different rates in different places, so one region turns from maze into coral while another breaks into spots. Pick any other `pattern` to hold one regime everywhere.

The concentration of B picks the glyph. In truecolor, dense interiors sit in deep teal, steep edges glow pale green, and growth from the last second flushes coral before it settles.

Drag to paint a trail of new growth. Press to drop a bloom that breaks up into the current pattern.

### Labyrinth in your theme

Mono without a phosphor follows `--ascii-ink` and `--ascii-paper`, so the maze draws in your site's colours in light and dark mode. ANSI blocks give it solid walls.

## Notes

- The canvas is `aria-hidden`; it is decoration.
- The reef grows 520 steps from scattered colonies before the first frame, so a still frame or a fresh resize already shows developed patterns. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` and with `animate={false}` it draws that frame and stops.
- Each frame runs up to 22 steps. At a 200 by 60 cell box that keeps the simulation and drawing within a few milliseconds.
- The reef wraps at the edges, so patterns never pile up against a border. When a patch dies out, a new colony sprouts in it within a couple of seconds.
- Switching between Braille and quadrants, or between charsets with one sample per glyph, keeps the reef. Switching between those two groups, a resize or a new `scale` regrows it at the new feature size.
