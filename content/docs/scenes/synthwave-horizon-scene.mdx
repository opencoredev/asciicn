## Usage

```tsx
import { SynthwaveHorizonScene } from "@/components/asciicn/scenes/synthwave-horizon-scene";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[520px]">
      <SynthwaveHorizonScene className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1 className="text-white">Night drive</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The ground is one surface: flat down the middle, rising into ridged mountains on both sides. Each sample marches its view ray until it lands, then draws grid lines one world unit apart where it hit. So the magenta floor grid climbs straight up the mountains as a cyan and violet wireframe, and ridges hide the lines behind them. Lines are measured against the size of one sample, so they stay one glyph wide at any distance and fade into the pink haze at the horizon instead of flickering.

The sun sits low in the gap between the ranges, yellow at the top and magenta at the bottom. Gaps open across its lower half, thin at first and wider as they slide down. Stars twinkle above it, the floor carries a faint reflection of the sun, and a soft bright band rolls down the screen like an old CRT.

Moving the pointer left and right turns the camera, with a slight bank; up and down tilts it. `travel` sets the driving speed and `mountains` the height of the ranges.

## Examples

### Edge glyphs

The `edge` charset draws `- / | \` along strong tone edges. Every grid line is an edge, so the floor turns into rows of dashes and fans of slashes converging on the sun, and the wireframe mountains into slanted strokes.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- With reduced motion turned on it draws one still frame, at `stillTime`, and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and gives up its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL2 it shows `fallback`, a gradient of the sun over the horizon.
- Rays down the flat corridor skip the march and hit the floor directly, so most of the cost is in the mountains, at up to 64 steps per sample. Braille samples eight times per glyph; a larger `cell` or a single-sample charset cuts the work. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
