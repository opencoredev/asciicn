## Usage

```tsx
import { CyberpunkRainScene } from "@/components/asciicn/scenes/cyberpunk-rain-scene";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[520px]">
      <CyberpunkRainScene className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1 className="text-white">After hours</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The default charset is Braille, which draws 2x4 dots per glyph, so window grids, masts and rim lights come out as fine lines.

The city is built in four layers. The far towers are tall, small and lost in haze; the near blocks are wide and dark, with cyan light down their left edges and magenta down their right, so every silhouette stays readable in any charset. Windows light at random, a few flicker, and tall towers carry masts with a blinking red beacon.

The two nearer layers hang neon signs: vertical banners of made-up characters, and the odd rooftop billboard. Signs flicker, some scroll, and now and then one tears sideways and swaps cyan for magenta. Below the kerb line the street mirrors the whole skyline, rippled by the rain and brighter where puddles collect, with rings opening where drops land. Three depths of rain fall in front, the nearest longest and fastest.

Ordered dithering is on by default (`dither="bayer4"`), so the haze behind the skyline draws as a field of glyphs that thins out with height, and the towers stand out as dark shapes against it, in every charset.

Moving the pointer shifts each layer by a different amount, nearer layers more, so the city has real depth. `fog`, `rain` and `wind` set the weather; `signs` and `glitch` set how busy the neon is.

Raising `rain` and `fog` softens the skyline, and a dark gradient over the lower third keeps the headline readable on top of the street reflections.

### Katakana on green phosphor

`charset="katakana"` swaps the dots for half-width katakana, and setting `phosphor` switches to one green ink. The scene still reads in one colour because its depth comes from tone: dark towers against the haze, bright windows, signs and rain. The rain here blows left.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- With reduced motion turned on it draws one still frame, at `stillTime`, and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and gives up its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL2 it shows `fallback`, a gradient in the scene's night colours.
- The whole city is one GLSL function evaluated once per sample, so the cost does not grow with the number of signs or windows. Braille samples eight times per glyph, so it costs more than single-sample charsets on large screens. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
