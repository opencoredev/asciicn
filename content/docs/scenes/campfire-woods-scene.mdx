## Usage

```tsx
import { CampfireWoodsScene } from "@/components/asciicn/scenes/campfire-woods-scene";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[520px]">
      <CampfireWoodsScene className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1 className="text-white">Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The night is painted back to front: a sky with a faint band of the Milky Way, the moon and twinkling stars, a far ridge of small pines, the clearing, a middle row of pines, the fire, then the nearest pines framing the edges. The fire lights the inner edge of every trunk it faces, and the moon rims the other side in cool blue.

Moving the pointer shifts each row of pines by a different amount, so the woods gain depth, and the flames lean away from the pointer as if it were a breeze. Sparks rise in a cone over the fire and die out at their own heights. Fireflies wander slow looping paths and glow in short pulses.

Pine silhouettes are cut out of the sky texture, so the scene reads in every charset. `dense` and `braille` give the finest flames; `blocks` and `quadrants` give a chunky poster look.

## Examples

### Behind a headline

The upper third is sky, so a headline sits there on quiet glyphs. `dense` gives the flames more steps, more `fireflies` fill the clearing, and less `smoke` keeps the middle clear. The paper stays a night colour in both site themes, so the text is white.

### Amber terminal

A `phosphor` switches to `mono`: one amber ink on dark glass, with the scene carried by glyph density alone. `blocks` and 14px cells make it chunky, and `CRT_FX` adds scanlines, bloom and curvature.

## Notes

- It is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws the `stillTime` frame and stops. It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen.
- The pointer is read from the window, so it works under overlays and text; the canvas itself never takes pointer events.
- One GPU pass draws every layer: there are no particle arrays on the CPU. Sparks and fireflies come from hashed grids, so their cost doesn't grow with their number. See [Performance](/docs/engine/performance).
