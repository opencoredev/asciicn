```tsx
  return (
    <div className="relative h-[520px] overflow-hidden rounded-lg">

}
```

The board starts with a few chips, each a heavy box-drawn package with pins on every side and a silkscreen label such as `U3 DSP`. Copper traces grow out of free pins one cell at a time. A trace runs straight, then turns through a 45° chamfer or a hard 90° corner, steers away from other copper, and sometimes tees off a branch. When it runs out of room or length it ends in a via (`○`) or a pad (`□`).

Electrons race along finished traces as bright pulses with fading tails, and the via they reach flares. Every few seconds one chip powers up: its traces brighten, fire more pulses and cast a cyan glow on the board around them. Traces dim with age and later retract from the tip, which frees their pins for new growth. Every 14 to 26 seconds the oldest chip retracts all its copper and lifts off, and a new one lands somewhere else.

Hover to power the chips near the pointer. Traces grow from the nearest free pins toward it, and the finished trace closest to it sprouts a branch in its direction. Click to drop a new chip under the pointer. Any copper in its footprint is cleared.

The `charset` prop sets the board's grain and glow: Braille dot haze by default, block shades with `blocks`, slashes with `edge`. `standard` also switches traces, chips and vias to plain ASCII (`-|+/\`, `o`, `#`), so the whole frame is 7-bit text.

## Notes

- The canvas is `aria-hidden`; it is decoration.
- The board grows for 24 seconds before the first frame, so a still frame or a fresh resize already shows routed copper. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` and with `animate={false}` it draws that frame and stops.
- Glyphs are written straight into the frame, so `invert`, `threshold`, `edgeThreshold` and `dither` have no effect.
- All board state lives in typed arrays sized once per grid, and colours come from a lookup table built when the look changes, so a frame allocates nothing. A 200 by 60 grid draws in well under 8 ms.
- A resize, a new `density` or a new `seed` grows a new board. Switching `charset` or colours keeps the current one.
