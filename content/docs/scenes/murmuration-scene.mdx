## Usage

```tsx
import { MurmurationScene } from "@/components/asciicn/scenes/murmuration-scene";

export function Dusk() {
  return (
    <div className="relative h-[520px] overflow-hidden rounded-lg">
      <MurmurationScene className="absolute inset-0" />
    </div>
  );
}
```

Each bird is a boid. It matches the heading of the birds near it, steers toward their centre and keeps a short distance from the closest ones. Birds sort into a grid of cells as wide as their sight, so each one checks only the birds in the cells around it, never the whole flock. Over that, a slow attractor drifts across the sky and the flock circles it. The circling changes direction every so often and a shear wind drifts through, which pull the flock into ribbons, sheets and tight balls.

Every frame each bird lands in the tone field at the charset's sample grid, and birds that share a sample add up. In Braille a lone bird is one dot, while a knot of hundreds fills cells solid. Ramp charsets shade each cell by how many birds are in it, from `.` to `@`. Reeds sway in front of a tree line along the bottom.

In truecolor the paper is a glowing dusk. The sky is stippled deep indigo at the top and thins out toward the horizon, and birds and land draw in near-black, so dense knots go dark the way they do against a real sunset. On a dark background, in mono with a phosphor or in your dark theme, it flips: the sky glows faintly toward the horizon, the land stays dim and the birds take the full ink. Ramp charsets draw the sky as a sparse scatter of light glyphs, so it never turns into rows of one character.

The pointer is a hawk. Birds near it flee, and the scare passes from bird to bird, weaker at each step, so a wave of jinking, darker birds runs out through the flock. Press to dive: the hawk folds its wings and clears a much wider hole. While the pointer is away, a hawk crosses the sky every 17 seconds.

## Examples

### In your theme

Mono without a phosphor follows `--ascii-ink` and `--ascii-paper`, so the flock draws in your site's colours in light and dark mode. The dense ramp shades each cell by how many birds are in it.

## Notes

- The canvas is `aria-hidden`; it is decoration.
- The flock flies five seconds ahead before the first frame, so a still frame or a fresh resize already shows a formed murmuration. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` and with `animate={false}` it draws that frame and stops.
- Steps are fixed at 1/30 s, up to three per frame. Each bird reacts to at most 9 neighbours and looks at no more than 48, so a step costs the same however tight the flock gets. All buffers are allocated on resize, never per frame.
- A resize or a new `birds` count starts a new flock. Switching charset or colours keeps it flying.
